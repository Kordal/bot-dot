# botdot for Android

A native Kotlin app (Jetpack Compose) for your botdot. It talks to botdot through Tailscale, the same way your browser does, using the API in [../docs/api.md](../docs/api.md).

## What it does

- **Your bots** as heads, each in its colour with its hat: the Chief of Staff and the bots you pinned as big heads on top, then the rest by category, each with its last message and when. A bot with new replies shows dots after its time, one per reply and at most three, in its colour (a big head shows them under it); the list stays in your order. A head's face shows what its bot is doing: it looks ahead and blinks when idle, moves its eyes from side to side under two brows while it works, looks to one side while it waits for a teammate, has wide eyes and bobs when it needs you, has crosses for eyes and tilts when a run failed, and has closed eyes and is faded when its jobs are switched off. With animations off in Android's settings nothing moves, and each head keeps its state's face. Press and hold a bot, then drag it to reorder it, move it to another category, or above the big heads to pin it; press and hold a big head to move it among them (unpin a bot in its settings). Tap a category's heading to fold it (the phone remembers); folded, a green dot beside it says one of its bots is working. **Search**, **＋** (a new chat with the Chief) and **Settings** are the round buttons on top.
- **Groups: a team in one chat.** In a bot's chat a small **＋** right of its name ("Add a bot to this chat" for a screen reader) opens a picker: tick one to five more bots, give the group a name, **Create**. The bot's own chat stays as it is. A group is one row in its category, after the category's bots (a pinned group stands after the pinned bots): its members' heads with the faces of their states, its name, its newest reply with the bot's name before it ("Coder: …"), dots for new replies, "Coder asks: …" with the round "?" when a member asks you something, and while a member works who it is and what it says it is doing. Press and hold it to drag or pin it, as a bot. In its chat every reply has its bot's name above it in the bot's colour and its head beside it. You write once and botdot gives the turns; typing **@** suggests the members, and **＋** attaches files. Under the chat stands one line per working member ("Coder · Working…") and one **Stop all**; a message that waits for a busy member is faded and says for whom ("Waits until Coder and Reviewer are free"), with **Take back**; when the group stopped after 12 replies without you the chat says so, with **Go on**. New replies have the **New** line, and the group is read while it is open and in front. A card asked in the group is at the end of its chat with its bot's name, and in Needs you ("Coder in Dev"). The group's title opens its settings: its name, its members each with ✕ to take it out, **＋ Add a bot**, and **Delete this group**, which deletes no bot. A reply in a group never notifies; a card and a question asked in a group do, with the group's name and the bot's, and a tap opens the group. botdot decides all of it: against a botdot from before groups none of this is offered, and no group is listed.
- **On a wide screen** (an unfolded phone, a tablet) the list stands beside the chat; the chat's first button hides or shows the list.
- **The keypad, from 840 dp wide** (a landscape tablet, an open foldable, a desktop window): the list becomes a keypad beside the chat. Needs you and the Board's line stay on top; under them every page stands one under the other (Pinned, each category A to Z, Other), its name over its keys with how many it holds and a dot in a bot's colour when one there asks you something. A page's bots and groups are keys in rows of three, in the list's order, each with its head (a group's with up to three), its name and the line its row would say; the first nine are numbered 1 to 9 across the pages. What doesn't fit scrolls, and the last place, **Ask the Chief for a bot**, opens a new chat with the Chief. The Chief's chat is on the right; a key opens its chat there and stays lit, and Back brings the Chief back. With a keyboard: 1 to 9 open a key, Esc brings the Chief back and Ctrl+K searches; a digit typed in a text field stays there. Search shows what the list's search shows, in place of the keys.
- **New replies:** a chat with new replies opens at a thin **New** line where they begin, and the line stays until you leave the chat. While a chat is on screen and the app is in front, the app tells botdot, which counts the chat as read: its dots go here and in the web page and the Mac app. With the app in the background or the screen off nothing is read. botdot keeps what you have read; against a botdot from before this, there are no dots and no line.
- **Chats with a bot or the Chief:** send a task, see in one line what it is about to do while it works, stop it, and read the reply with Markdown formatting. A message that could not be sent is back in the box with its files, under a line that says why; a quick answer stays to be tapped again. Sent again as it is, it arrives once: botdot takes each message once, also when only its answer was lost. A reply that failed after writing shows what it wrote and, under it, what went wrong. The Chief's chat has **＋** for a new chat and its earlier chats.
- **Files:** **＋** beside the message box, or **Share → botdot** from any app, to the Chief or any bot.
- **Notifications with no push service.** The app keeps one quiet connection open to botdot, and opens it again after the phone restarts. You're notified of a new reply, as botdot counts them: a bot's answer or report to you, a run that didn't finish, the Chief's replies, and never one in a chat you're looking at in any app. Each chat's messages are stacked in one notification with **Reply**, and show the bot's head with its hat. With high priority, you're notified of what waits for you: a login or payment, a job, a permission, skill or tool, a memory change or a plan (with **Decline** / **Approve** right on the notification), and Bitwarden being locked. Each says what waits in botdot's own words. Where the yes needs more than a notification can show (a script of a job or a tile, a tool from a link), it has only **Decline**: you say yes on its card, which shows the script, or the warning, the address and the checksum. An ask the app has no words for says "Needs your OK" with no button, and a tap opens its card. The notification of a Chief reply that failed says what went wrong. Reading a chat, here, on the web page or on the Mac, clears its notification, and the app icon's dot goes with the last one. After a dropped connection it picks up where it left off, and after a restart the notifications match what botdot says is new, without ringing again. (A botdot from before new replies is followed as before: every reply to you notifies, except in the chat on screen or while you use the web page.)
- **Needs you:** a row on top of your bots when something waits, with one small head per thing, in its bot's colour and hat, and everything in one place:
  - **a bot asking for a login:** let it use one you saved, or save a new one, as a login, an API token or any other secret (the card opens with the type the bot asked for, and its fields follow the type); it goes straight into the vault or Bitwarden, and the bot never sees the password
  - **a bot wanting to pay** with a saved card
  - **unlocking Bitwarden** with your master password, kept only in botdot's memory
  - **jobs the Chief or a bot set up**, with the script shown, to approve, always approve or turn down
  - **memory changes** waiting for your OK
  - **a permission or skill** a bot asked for, and **a bot's plan for the Chief**
  - **a tool to install** on the botdot server for one bot: what it is and why, who asked, and its facts (version, source, size), with **Decline** and **Install**; a tool from a link has a warning in amber, with the link, the file and its checksum. After **Install** the card says "Installing", and the Chief tells you in its chat how it went

  The same cards also show at the end of the chat they belong to (a bot's own, or the Chief's; a tool to install in both), so you can approve right there; logins and payments open this screen.

  This screen can't be screenshotted, since passwords are typed there, and its password fields work with your password manager's autofill. Its notifications open it directly.
- **After your OK the work carries on.** A card says in one line who carries on when you allow it ("It carries on with “Check why the backup failed last night.” when you allow it.", or "The Chief carries on in this chat when you allow it."), and once answered it says so again: "Allowed · it carries on". The buttons keep their words, on the cards and in the notifications; what follows an Allow from a notification arrives as the bot's or the Chief's next notification. A change to a bot that the Chief asked for also stands in the Chief's chat, where you asked for it, with a title that names what changes; a changed text shows the old text struck and the new one under it. An action in a connected app shows what it sends as rows (To, Subject, Text) and, under its title, that the bot waits for your answer or when it stopped waiting. While a bot waits for it, its chat says "Waiting for your OK" where its line stood, with a still dot. botdot decides all of it and the app shows its words: against a botdot from before this, every card and line is as it was.
- **Busy and stopped.** The message box keeps **Send** while a bot works or the Chief replies. Your message then waits its turn: it stands at the end of the chat, faded, with "Waits until Fleet Doctor is free" and **Take back**, which puts its text and files in the box again ("It has already started." when you were too late). **Stop** is the word at the end of the working line, in a bot's chat and in the Chief's, where that line takes the place of the "Thinking…" bubble. A run that was ended says who ended it, in botdot's sentence ("You stopped this run before it finished.", "The Chief stopped this run before it finished."), and so does a Chief reply; a result that arrives late is a Chief reply with the chip "↩ From Web Surfer". Under a job stands botdot's own line ("Did not run at 08:00: botdot was off."), **Run now** says whether the job started, waits for the bot or was skipped, and a Board tile that asks a busy bot says "Home Keeper will do it when it is free". While Claude is signed out, one line above the message box says so and leads to **Settings**, and the phone gets one notification, also while you use the web page. Against a botdot from before this, every screen is as it was: Stop in the message box while something works.
- **Nothing approved unseen, no question lost.** With several things waiting, the button on top of Needs you counts what it approves ("Approve 3"): AI jobs and shared-memory changes, never a change to what you said or a forget. A job or tile that runs a script is left for its own card and named in one line ("Disk check runs a script: approve it on its card."). A new job's no says what it does, in the botdot server's words ("Decline and remove", then "Declined and removed"), on the card and in the notification. A change to a job or tile you approved has a card of its own, "Morning news: a change": the approved time beside the new one, the approved text struck above the new, both scripts where the script changes, and "Runs as you approved it until you answer."; **Keep as it is** drops the change and removes nothing. "Always allow this action" asks before it answers. A bot that asks you something reads "Asks: …" in the list, with a round "?" in its colour in place of the dots, until you answer it; its notification is titled "Mail Sorter asks".
- **Scheduled jobs** (⏰ in a bot's chat): pause or resume each one with its switch, run it now, and see when it runs next and how its last run went.
- **A bot's settings** (tap its name in its chat, saved with **Save**): name, colour, what it does, category, pinning and its main assignment; then its own computer, learning, scheduled jobs, skills, apps, who it may message, and the jobs you always approve. Model and reasoning, and keeping its computer on are under **Advanced**. Delete a bot here, too.
- **The Chief's settings** (tap its name in its chat): its model and reasoning, your rules for it, and its earlier chats. Two switches, each saved when you tap it: **Checkup**, with botdot's words for its days and time under it ("Weekdays at 08:00. Ask the Chief to change it."), and **Web search**, which lets a checkup search the web and can't be used while Checkup is off. A checkup's message opens a new Chief chat with its answer buttons ("Do 1", "Do all", "Not now") and no 👍 and 👎. Against a botdot from before checkups neither switch is shown.
- **Memory has no screen.** You ask a bot what it remembers, and correct, remove, forget and bring back by chat ([Memory](../docs/user-guide.md#memory-pinned-notes-searchable-notes-and-past-runs)). The app shows two things only, both in the botdot server's own words, the same as on the web page: the chip under a reply, printed whole, which says what was saved, changed, removed or forgotten and opens nothing; and the card of a memory change that waits for your OK, in Needs you and at the end of its bot's chat, with its notification. A change is one block, the old text struck above the new. A change to something you said yourself, and **Forget for good**, each have a card of their own: they are never part of "Approve N" nor counted on it, and their notification has no button, a tap opens the card. A skill a bot wrote after reading content from others waits on a card with the whole text your yes would save. Against a botdot from before this, chips and cards read as they did.
- **Its screen** (the screen button in its chat, or **See its screen** in its settings, for bots with a computer): watch its desktop live, or **Take control** to use it yourself.
- **Settings** (⚙ on the home screen): the default model and reasoning, the Claude sign-in (sign out or use another account), botdot's version with **Check for updates** and **Update now**, backups, **Sign out everywhere** behind a front door (every browser and app signed in to your account signs in again), and links to **Skills**, **Connectors**, **Notifications in the background** (**Notifications are off** with **Turn on** instead, when they are) and changing botdot's address.
- **Skills:** your library, which bots use each skill, and what it tells them.
- **Connectors:** your connected apps, and connecting new ones; their sign-in opens in the phone's browser.

Android shows a small permanent "Connected" notification for the open connection, which you can hide in the app's notification settings. So the phone doesn't cut it off while asleep, use **Settings → Notifications in the background** and allow it.

## Install on your phone

The app is built on GitHub once a night when it changed (or when the owner runs **Actions → Android → Run workflow** on `main`) and published on the repo's **Releases** page as **botdot for Android 0.1.x**. The server image, built after it the same night, carries the newest one too, so once botdot is updated the app updates itself: **A new version of the app is ready · Install** shows on the home screen, and **Settings → Updates** shows **This phone · Update available · Update**. Install downloads it from your botdot over Tailscale and opens Android's installer. The first time, Android asks you to allow installing apps from botdot. Your settings stay.

The first install (or if something goes wrong): open the Releases page on your phone (signed in to GitHub), download the `.apk` and install it.

All builds are signed with the same release key, so they update each other. The key lives in the repo's secrets and in `~/.config/bot-dot/` on the computer that made it. Keep that folder backed up: without the key, the next version can only be installed after removing the app.

## Build and install

It needs JDK 17 or newer (Android Studio's bundled one works) and the Android SDK with platform 37.

```bash
cd android
./gradlew assembleDebug      # for testing (its own debug key)
./gradlew assembleRelease    # signed with the release key from ~/.config/bot-dot/android-signing.properties
adb install -r app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk
```

A debug build and a release build are signed differently, so switching between them means removing the app first.

Or open the `android` folder in Android Studio and press Run.

When the app first opens, enter your botdot's address, e.g. `my-vm.my-tailnet.ts.net`, with Tailscale on. Behind a front door ([ADR 028](../docs/adr/028-botdot-serves-several-accounts-on-one-machine-each-walled-off.md)) the app then signs in once with your account's passkey, through Android's own sheet, and stays signed in until it is signed out; the front door must name the app's signing key in `FRONT_DOOR_ANDROID_KEYS` (docs/api.md, "The front door"). A debug build is signed with your own debug key, so name that one too to sign in from it.

**Checks** (CI runs them too): `./gradlew testDebugUnitTest` (unit tests) and `./gradlew lintDebug` (new lint problems fail; the ones already there are in `app/lint-baseline.xml`). In debug builds each request to botdot shows in Logcat under the tag `botdot-http`.

**Baseline profile** (faster start and scrolling): `app/src/release/generated/baselineProfiles` holds the code a start and the first steps run, compiled when the app is installed. After bigger changes to the screens, make it again with an emulator or phone connected (Android 13 or later) and commit it:

```bash
./gradlew :app:generateReleaseBaselineProfile -Pandroid.testInstrumentationRunnerArguments.server=http://10.0.2.2:4747
./gradlew :baselineprofile:connectedBenchmarkReleaseAndroidTest   # cold start, without and with it (on a real phone)
```

**The head:** the app draws bots from the botdot server's own definition, `../lib/figure.json` (the head, the face and movement of each state, where the hat sits and the kit of hats; see [ADR 003](../docs/adr/003-one-figure-definition-for-all-apps.md) and [ADR 004](../docs/adr/004-pieces-as-restricted-shape-lists.md)). The build copies that file into the app (`copyFigure` in `app/build.gradle.kts`), so there is no second copy in Git; once connected, the app asks the server for its definition (`GET /api/figure`) and draws from that instead. `data/Figure.kt` reads a definition and says what to draw and where its three layers (head, eyes, pupils) are at a moment, `ui/BotFigure.kt` draws it, and `ui/FigureClock.kt` asks for frames only while something moves; `public/figure.js` on the web page is the reference they must match.

**Testing against a botdot on your computer:** the emulator reaches your computer as `10.0.2.2`. Start botdot with `BOT_DOT_ALLOWED_HOSTS=10.0.2.2`, then connect the app to `http://10.0.2.2:4747`. Plain HTTP is allowed for that address only, and only in debug builds.

## Layout

```
app/src/main/java/dev/botdot/app/
  BotDotApp.kt        the app's shared pieces: settings, the API, the live feed, notifications
  MainActivity.kt     navigation, notification taps, Share → botdot
  data/Api.kt         botdot's API over OkHttp, with live streams (Server-Sent Events)
  data/Models.kt      what the API returns
  data/Unread.kt      new replies: how many dots, where the "New" line goes, and the report of the chat on screen
  data/Figure.kt      the bots' head, read from the server's definition: what to draw for a state and a hat
  data/Store.kt       what several screens show (bots, the Chief's chats, what waits for you, the Board), kept
                      once and brought up to date from the live feed, only the parts each event changes
  data/Settings.kt    the saved botdot address, and which categories you folded
  data/AppUpdate.kt   installing a newer app from your botdot
  feed/Feed.kt        the one open connection to /api/events, reconnecting and resuming by itself
  feed/FeedService.kt keeps that connection open in the background
  feed/Notifier.kt    turns feed events into notifications
  feed/Receivers.kt   the notifications' buttons, and reconnecting after a restart
  ui/                 the screens (connect, bots, a bot's chat and settings, its screen, the Chief and its settings,
                      share, needs you, jobs, settings, skills, connectors), the memory section, the app
                      update row, Markdown, form pieces, the bots' heads, the Paper theme
  ui/ChatModels.kt    a bot's chat and a Chief chat, kept while open (and while the phone turns): newest part first,
                      older as you scroll up
app/src/test/         unit tests
baselineprofile/      makes the baseline profile, and the start-up benchmark
```
