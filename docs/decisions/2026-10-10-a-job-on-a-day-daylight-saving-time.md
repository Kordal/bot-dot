2026-10-10: A job on a day daylight saving time moves the clock follows classic cron (`cron.due` in `lib/cron.js`), because a job at a fixed time ran twice when the clock went back and not at all when it went forward, and classic cron's rule is the one a reader of a schedule knows. A job at a fixed time, whose minute and hour are not `*`, starts once: in the hour the clock repeats only at the first copy of its time, and a time the clock jumps over starts at the jump (local 03:00 in Europe/Warsaw). A job whose minute or hour is `*` goes by the clock as before: in both copies of a repeated hour, none in a skipped one. As in classic cron, a minute or hour counts as `*` only when it starts with one: `0 0-23 * * *` is a fixed time and starts 24 times on the fall-back day. `cron.next` (a job's next run and the schedule preview) and the missed times of `missedWhileOff` follow the same rule, and the minutes are counted in time, not on the clock, so the second copy of an hour is not read as the first. The scheduler still keeps its last look as a UTC minute, so a restart in another time zone starts nothing extra. A catch-up is decided at the jump minute only: a look that misses that minute misses it, as any other missed minute. Rejected: a key of the local minute (it would also keep every-minute jobs from the second copy); running the second copy instead of the first; moving a skipped time to the same minute of the next hour.
