2026-11-24: a job the owner asked for without a clock time ("in the morning", "every evening", "daily") has `around: true` and runs at a random minute within ±15 minutes of its cron time, a different minute each day; one the owner gave a time ("at 8", "at 7:30") runs exactly then, because the owner chose that window: when the Chief and the bots all wrote `0 8 * * *`, every bot ran at 8:00 and the owner got a burst of notifications at once. The model does not pick the minute (asked for a random one it keeps choosing the same), the server does: the offset is a hash of (job id, local date of the time), so a restart gives the same minute, and the one set of runs that `cron.due`, `cron.next` and `lastDueBetween` all read keeps a run from firing twice or being lost, a run moved before its time by a negative offset and a change of the clock included. Rejected: a random minute stored on the job at creation (the same minute every day, and the bursts only spread out once); a random minute drawn at each tick (not stable across a restart, and the catch-up after downtime could not tell what was missed); ±30 minutes (a "morning" job at 8:00 reaching 7:30 or 8:30 leaves the part of the day the owner meant); setting it for every job, or for a schedule that is not at a fixed time of day (every 15 minutes) or a one-time `at` job, which run on the minute. It is part of what a job does, so changing it waits for the owner's OK as a change of time does. A page's "Keep up to date" schedule is picked by the owner on the sheet with a time, so it stays exact. ([#1667](https://github.com/Kordal/bot-dot/issues/1667))
