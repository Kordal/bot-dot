# User guide

How botdot works, from the app to every feature. To install it, see [Install on Linux](install-linux.md). What each bot can reach on a Docker install is in [What bots can reach](what-bots-can-reach.md).

A messenger-style app for a team of **bots**. Each bot is a headless Claude Code agent with its own instructions, tools, permissions, workspace and scheduled jobs, and a Chief of Staff creates and runs them for you.

**Apps:** besides the web page, there's botdot for Android ([android/](../android/README.md)). On a computer, use the web page. The Android app is built by GitHub Actions, and botdot's image carries the newest one, so phones update themselves from your botdot. The Mac app is paused and not maintained ([ADR 031](adr/031-the-mac-app-is-stopped-again.md)): it was never released, and the image carries none.

## The app

botdot looks like a messenger: every bot is a head in its own colour in the sidebar, wearing a hat that fits its job, with the Chief of Staff at the top. A bot's face shows what it is doing: it looks ahead and blinks when it has nothing to do, looks from side to side under straight brows while it works, looks away while it waits for teammates, has wide eyes and bobs when it needs you, has crosses for eyes and a tilted head when it couldn't finish, and has closed eyes and is faded when it is paused. Beside a bot's name in text there is a small still head with its hat. A bot with new replies shows a dot for each after its time, up to three, in its colour; its chat opens at a "New" line above the first of them, and looking at the chat takes the dots away in every app. Bots you pin (in a bot's settings, or by dragging) come right below it, in your own order.
- **A wide window** (wider than 1000 px) opens on the **keypad** in place of the list: the Board's values and Needs you on top, then all your pages one under the other (pinned, each category A to Z, Other), each with its bots and groups as keys in rows of three, each with its head, name and line. The Chief's chat stays open on the right; a key opens its chat there instead, and **Esc** brings the Chief back. **1**–**9** open the first nine keys, counted across the pages, and **Ctrl+K** searches; a digit typed into a message stays in the message. A narrower window shows the list ([ADR 029](adr/029-a-wide-window-opens-on-the-keypad-with-one-chat-on-the-right.md)).
- **Messaging a bot** gives it a task. Its runs appear as a conversation: your task, then its reply, with the time and how long it took underneath. **Details** shows every step, and the bot's live screen appears while it works. A bot does one thing at a time: a message you send while it works waits its turn, faded at the end of the chat until the bot is free, and **Take back** puts it into the message box again. **Stop** is the word at the end of its working line; the Chief's chat has the same line and the same Stop.
- **Attaching files:** 📎 in the message box, or drop or paste files onto the chat (up to 10 at a time, 25 MB each). They're saved in that bot's workspace (`uploads/`), or the Chief's, and the bot can read them even without the Read tool. Text, Markdown, CSV, JSON, PDF and images are read as they are; for Excel, Word, PowerPoint and OpenDocument files botdot also saves a readable copy (each sheet as CSV, documents and slides as text). Small text files go straight into the message.
- **Quoting a message:** select text in one message of a chat (yours or a bot's, in a bot's chat, a group or the Chief's) and tap **Quote**: a button by the selection on the web page, an item of the selection menu in the Android app. The text goes into the message box as a Markdown quote (`> ` in front of each line), after what you typed already, with the cursor on a new line under it; the bot gets it as part of your message. A selection over two messages has no Quote.
- **Main assignment:** what a bot does when a job runs it without a task of its own. A message you type is handled in the bot's role, with its main assignment as the guide, e.g. "any news?" means news in its area. A new bot's empty chat shows its head, its name and what it does: to run its main assignment, ask it.
- **Setup** opens by itself on first start, and whenever Claude isn't signed in: a checklist with **Sign in to Claude** (open the sign-in page, paste the code back), the bots' computers, your phone (Tailscale), the time zone schedules use (with one click to use your device's), the optional extra (Bitwarden), and, the first time, **Name your Chief of Staff**: leave it empty to keep "Chief of Staff". The Android app asks the same question once, when you open it before setup is done.
- **⚙ Settings**, at the bottom of the sidebar, opens **Activity** (every run, with how many are running), **Skills**, **Connectors** and the **Vault**, and holds the default model, the Claude sign-in and backups. The admin updates botdot on the admin page. To make a new bot, ask the Chief of Staff.
- **Needs you:** everything waiting for your OK (jobs, permissions or skills a bot asked for, memory changes, logins, payments, a locked Bitwarden) shows as a banner of small heads, one per item in its bot's colour and hat, with **Review**. When a bot's settings refuse something it tried (the web, changing files, running commands), botdot asks you for that permission itself, with what it tried; after a no, it doesn't ask for it again for a week. Harmless read-only commands (like `ls` or `find`) are let through by Claude Code without the permission.
- **A bot's settings** (⚙ in its chat) are a page of their own, and every change saves by itself. Up front: name, colour, what it does, its category and main assignment, then one line each for its computer (a screen of its own on the account's computer), pinning, skills, apps, who it may message, its scheduled jobs and the jobs you always approve. Its memory has no section here: you ask the bot ([Memory](#memory-pinned-notes-searchable-notes-and-past-runs)). Everything technical is folded under **Advanced**: what it may do (change files, browse the web, run commands, or the exact tools and permission mode), model and reasoning, and personality / rules.
- **Scheduled jobs** have their own page too (from the bot's settings): pick a time from the list or **Another time…** for any cron schedule; the kind (AI or script) and a script's time limit are under **More options**.
- **Search:** the search box above the bots also finds messages in every chat (yours, the bots' replies, the Chief's and those between bots). Matches show under the bots with the matching words in bold; click one to open that chat at that message. While the search box has text, the words you searched for are marked in yellow in the chat you open.
- **On a phone**, you get the contact list first; tap a contact to open the chat.
- **Notifications in a browser:** **⚙ Settings → Notifications on this device** turns on the notifications the Android app shows (replies, a bot's question, and what needs you) for the browser you are in: on a computer, on Android, and on an iPhone or iPad. The browser asks once whether botdot may show notifications. On an iPhone or iPad, add botdot to the Home Screen first (Share → **Add to Home Screen**), open it from there, and turn the switch on in its Settings: Safari's tab gets none. Your botdot server sends them itself, encrypted for that browser, through the browser maker's push service (Apple's, Google's or Mozilla's), which can't read them. A tap opens the chat or the card. Turn the switch off to stop them on that device.
- **Design:** the "Mono" theme is dark only, on the web page and on Android, whatever your system's setting. The mockups it was chosen from are in [design/mockups.html](../design/mockups.html).

## What it does

**Chief of Staff** (at the top of the sidebar) is a chat that manages your other bots. If you gave it a name in setup, the apps show that name instead, everywhere: the list, its chat, mentions (`@Ada`), cards and notifications. To change its name later, ask it ("call yourself Max from now on", or "go back to Chief of Staff"); there is no setting for it. `@ChiefOfStaff` still reaches it too. A bot can't be called by the Chief's name, nor "Chief of Staff". Describe what you need and it will:
- create new bots from the conversation, choosing the task, tools, permissions and scheduled jobs
- set **reminders** for you ("remind me Friday at 9 to renew the token"): at that time a ⏰ message appears in the chat, with a notification, and no bot runs for it. Ask it to list or cancel them
- change existing bots, run them, wait for their results and summarise them. A bot that finishes after the Chief stopped waiting (a long task, or one you told it not to wait for) posts its reply in that Chief chat by itself, as **Message from Web Surfer** (with the bot's small head before its name), with a notification
- ask before deleting a bot or running something with lasting effects
- **write to you first**, when it has something tied to what you asked about: a follow-up, a tip, a fresh find from the web, or a joke. A few times a day, outside quiet hours, the botdot server gives the Chief a moment to look around; in it the Chief can only read (your chats, your bots and their runs, the shared memory, the web) and changes nothing. It ends with one message at the end of the Chief chat you last used, shown and notified like any reply, or with nothing, which leaves no trace. At most three such messages a day, none in the first hour after botdot starts. Each has 👍 and 👎 under it, and the Chief sees your ratings and answers the next time it looks. There are no controls for this in the apps: tell the Chief "write less", "at most once a day", "stop writing first" or "no jokes" (its `writing_first` tool; a lasting preference becomes a shared-memory note)

It controls botdot through a small built-in MCP server (`lib/mcp-server.js`). It can't change files itself, and it can never give a bot `bypassPermissions`; only you can set that, in the bot's settings (Advanced → Exact tools and permission mode). Chats are saved in `data/chief/`, and each one continues the same Claude Code session.

**Bots** each run as `claude -p` in their own workspace, `data/workspaces/<bot-id>/`. It's a separate folder with its own empty git repository, so bots never see botdot's code. Each bot has:
- a main assignment (placeholders: `{{date}}`, `{{datetime}}`, `{{bot}}`), which also guides how it handles messages you send it
- its chat as you saw it, included when you message it, so follow-ups work: your last four exchanges with it and the last two that the Chief, a teammate or a scheduled job started, from the last seven days, each under the name of who asked. Its newest reply is there whole, older ones are cut and can be read in full, and a tapped answer arrives with the question it answers. Runs with nothing to report are left out
- personality or rules, added to what botdot tells it on every run. That text also holds its notes and its chat, so botdot hands it to Claude Code in a private file, never on the command line ([SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md))
- allowed tools (`--allowedTools`) and a permission mode (`dontAsk` is the safe default, meaning anything not allowed is denied)
- an optional model per bot
- any number of scheduled jobs (see below), which run while the server is running

A fresh install starts with just the Chief; ask it for the bots you need.

**Categories:** give bots a category such as *Home* or *Work*: pick or create one in a bot's settings, drag the bot onto a category's heading (or onto a bot in that category; *Other* removes it from its category), or ask the Chief. The sidebar then lists them under those headings, with bots without a category under *Other*. Fold a category by clicking its heading (remembered in this browser); its **⋯** menu renames or removes the category. Removing it only moves its bots to Other.

## Board

The **Board** is a page of tiles for the things you use most: a light switch, the temperature, a server's disk, your next calendar items, a morning summary. The strip at the top of the sidebar (and of the Android list), above the Chief of Staff, shows its tiles' names and values and opens it. Ask the Chief ("add a switch for the living room light", "move Servers to the top", "make the summary wide") and it builds them; there are no edit buttons. **Drag tiles** to move them where you like: among the others, under another heading, onto a group to put them in it, or out of an open group (on Android press, hold and drag). Right-click a tile (on Android hold it without moving) to remove it. While the Board is empty, the strip reads "Board · Nothing on it yet", and the page says in two lines what it is for and what to tell the Chief or a bot.

- **Tiles:** a **switch** (its dot is filled when on, a ring when off; tap to switch), a **button** (tap to do one thing), a **value** (a number or a few words, with a green, amber or red dot when it has limits; tap to refresh), a **list** (up to 8 lines) and a **note** (a few short lines). A list or note shows its first 3 lines; tap it and all of it opens in a small pop-up, with a link to its bot's chat, so the other tiles stay where they are. Tiles in a row keep their own height. Tiles sit under short headings, and lists and notes can be twice as wide.
- **Groups:** a **group** tile holds related tiles, like your lights under "Lights". It shows one dot per tile inside (filled when a light is on, a value's colour when it has limits); tap it and its tiles open right below it, tap again to fold them. Ask the Chief ("put my light tiles into one Lights card"). Removing a group asks whether to remove only the group, putting its tiles back loose, or the group with its tiles.
- **Where values come from:** best, an API call with a saved login, which botdot makes itself, with no AI, so a tap is instant. That's how Home Assistant works: a long-lived token in the Vault, and the Chief finds the entity and wires up `turn_on` / `turn_off`. Or a small script in the bot's workspace, which waits for your OK in **Needs you** first (and again when it changes), like a script job. Or the bot fills the tile itself (`set_tile`), for example from a morning job. A button can also send its bot a message, which starts a run.
- **Every tile belongs to a bot:** its saved logins and workspace are used, and its colour is the tile's dot. Deleting the bot removes its tiles.
- **Suggestions from bots:** a bot suggests a tile when you ask it to put something on the Board (first asking you one short question about what you want, like an on/off switch or a brightness slider, with buttons for the answers, unless your request leaves no doubt), or when it notices you keep asking it for the same action or value (during a run, or in its weekly memory review, which also sees the API calls it made). A suggestion shows faded under **Suggested** at the end of the Board, with the bot and why, and as a card at the end of that bot's chat. **Add** puts it on (that's your OK, script included); **✕** means that bot won't suggest it again. At most 3 wait at once.
- **Fresh while you look:** values are read again only while the Board is open, as often as you chose when the tile was set up (the bot or the Chief asks: every minute, every 15 minutes, hourly, daily, or only when you tap it; every minute by default), and right after a tap. A tile whose read is a script runs that script each time, so more often means more runs. A tile's pop-up says how often it refreshes; to change it, ask the Chief ("refresh the disk tile every hour"). Everything is in `data/dashboard.json`. The Chief follows its skill `chief:board`.

## Groups: several bots in one chat

A **group** is one chat with two to six bots, its **members**. You write once, every member reads it, and each answers under its own name or says nothing. A bot can be in several groups, and its own chat with you stays apart from all of them.

- **Making one:** in a bot's chat tap the small **+** right of its name (**Add a bot to this chat**), pick the others and give the group a name. Or ask the Chief ("put Coder, Reviewer and Issue Writer in a group called Dev"). The group appears in the list like a bot, in a category, with its members' heads as its picture. Tap the chat's title to rename it, add a bot, take one out or delete the group; deleting a group deletes no bot.
- **Who answers:** a message without a mention gives every member a **turn**, one after another in the group's order, each having read the replies before it. A member that has nothing of its own to add **passes**, which shows nothing. Every reply has its bot's name above it, in its colour, and its head beside it.
- **A mention picks who answers:** "@Coder which branch are you on?" gives the turn to Coder alone. The others read it later, as part of the chat. Everywhere else a mention only says what a message is about; in a group it gives the turn.
- **Handing on:** a member hands the work on by naming another member in its reply the same way ("@Reviewer, please look at the change"), and that member has the next turn. So you write "the settings page needs a dark mode switch" once, and the issue, the change and the review follow. It ends when a reply names nobody. A name written inside code hands nothing on.
- **The limit:** after 12 replies without a message from you the group stops and says so. **Go on**, tapped or typed, gives it 12 more; a new message of yours has a count of its own.
- **Busy and stopped:** a member that is working, here or in its own chat, is waited for, and the others go ahead; a message none of whose members has started shows as waiting, with **Take back**. Under the chat stands one line for each member at work and one **Stop all**, which ends them all and the turns that would have followed.
- **Cards and questions:** what a member needs your OK for is a card in **Needs you** and at the end of the group, with the bot's name on it. Another member's "go ahead" is a reply, never an OK; after your yes the member carries on in the group. A member's question shows as **Asks** on the group, and your next message gives that member the first turn.
- **New replies, without noise:** the group's row shows dots for its new replies and the chat a "New" line. Your phone rings only for a card or a question, never for a reply, and not for a question you have on screen as it arrives.
- **What a member reads:** the group's chat as you saw it, each entry saying who wrote it, its own notes and the shared ones. Not its private chat with you, and not another member's notes or runs. A file you attach is handed to each member that takes a turn on that message.
- **Safety:** another member's reply is a bot's text, never your instructions and never your OK. What a member saves in a turn that answers your message is your word; in a turn another member handed on it is not, and there a forget, or a change to something you said, waits for you on a card.

## Bots working together

A bot can message its teammates, like texting a colleague: hand off work another bot is better at, ask what it knows, or leave it a note.

- **Who may message whom** is up to you: **May message** in a bot's settings, or ask the Chief ("let Web Surfer only talk to Mail Assistant"). By default every bot may message every other bot, including ones you add later. A bot can always reply to the one that messaged it.
- **priority** starts the other bot right away, or as soon as it's free. Otherwise the message waits in its inbox and it reads it at the start of its next run.
- **Waiting for answers:** a bot that asks teammates something (with priority) doesn't wait around. Their answers collect in its inbox, and once everyone it asked has answered (or finished without answering, or after about 10 minutes, up to an hour for bots still working on it), botdot starts it once more with all the answers and the task they were for, so it can finish the job. It is started also when a teammate, or every teammate, sent no message back, and is then given what became of each: the reply the teammate wrote in its own chat, what went wrong when its run failed or was stopped, or that it has not finished yet. It finishes your task with what it has and tells you plainly what a teammate did not deliver. Meanwhile its head looks to one side. When the Chief handed it that task, its final reply also appears in that Chief chat as **Message from Fleet Doctor**, with its notification, and the Chief reads it the next time you write there. A bot started by a message also sees its own earlier run in that exchange.
- **In the chats:** messages between bots stay folded. A run another bot started shows as two lines, **Message from Web Surfer ›** and **Replied to Web Surfer ›** (each bot named has its small head before its name), that open to the message, what it sent back and its reply. The sender's reply is headed **Messaged Coder**. Notes waiting in a bot's inbox show at the bottom of its chat as one folded line, **Notes waiting: Web Surfer, Coder**, that opens to what each said. Once its next run reads them, they move above that run as **Read notes from …**.
- **Approving in the chat:** whatever a bot waits for your OK on (a job it set up, a permission or skill it asked for, a memory change, a login) also shows as a card at the end of its chat, and what the Chief set up or asked for at the end of the Chief's. Approve or decline it right there; it's gone from **Needs you** too, and the other way round. Declining a job or a tile that was never approved removes it, and its button says so: **Decline and remove**. Logins and payments open their secure pop-up.
- **After your yes, the work carries on by itself:** you don't send the request again. A bot that stopped because it lacked a permission or a skill is started again on the same request, told what you allowed, and so is a bot whose own proposal you approved (a job it added or changed, a tile it suggested, a change to its instructions); the Chief gets a turn in the chat where it asked for a changed bot, a skill, a script job or a Board tile, and does what waited for it. The card says so before you answer ("It carries on with … when you allow it."). Your yes only changes the setting for a request you have since replaced, by writing to that bot or in that Chief chat after it was refused (a later request that was refused the same carries on in its place), a day after the card appeared, or when the bot is gone; a job is not run again for a bot whose jobs are all switched off: nothing runs twice.
- **Suggestions for the Chief:** only the Chief creates and changes bots, jobs and logins, so a bot can't message it like a teammate. A bot can hand it a suggestion instead, like Bot Designer's finished plan for a new bot. It opens a new Chief chat that waits for you, and shows in **Needs you**: tap **Approve** or **Decline** on the card or in the chat, or answer in your own words in the chat.
- **No limits** on how many bots a run messages, how often, or how many suggestions a bot sends. The one safety net: a chain of hand-offs stops after 20 messages, so two bots can't answer each other forever.
- **Safety:**
  - A message is treated as information from a bot, never as your instructions. It can't grant logins, approve jobs or change anything a bot couldn't change anyway.
  - Runs started by a message count as having read outside content, so their shared-memory changes wait for your OK.
  - On Docker installs, each bot still can't see another's files.

## Scheduled jobs: AI only when it's needed

A bot can have many jobs, each with its own schedule (5-field cron, local time), task and on/off switch. A job can also run **just once** ("have Coder check the PRs tomorrow at 7"): it shows as *28 Sep at 07:00* and removes itself after it has run. There are two kinds:

- **🧠 AI jobs** are a normal run of the bot, with the job's own task, or its main assignment if the job has none.
- **⚙️ Script jobs** are a small bash script that runs **without AI**: free, fast and predictable. They fit mechanical work with a clear rule: is a site up, did a page or API result change, did a price cross a limit, disk space, backups. The script runs in the bot's folder, and `$JOB_STATE_DIR` keeps files between runs so it can compare with last time. A script can also **hand over to the AI** only when needed: when it prints something (the usual pattern: print nothing when there's nothing to report), when it fails, or every time. The AI then gets the script's output with the job's instructions.

When you ask the Chief or a bot for something on a schedule, its **first step is to decide whether it needs AI at all**. It records why (shown as 💡 on the job) and tells you which kind it picked. Quiet script runs (no output, no error) don't appear in the chat; the job just shows when it last ran. A scheduled script that hands its output to the AI doesn't appear either: the chat shows what the AI reports, and the script's output stays in the run's details. A script that fails appears, and one you start with **Run now** shows what it printed.

**A job that fails is told once.** The first failed run of a job on its schedule shows in the chat with what went wrong, a dot and a notification. While it keeps failing, the next runs are quiet: they are in the run history and counted on the job's line ("failed, 5 times in a row since 02:00"), and a run that works again ends the row. A first failure during quiet hours is held: you are told once when quiet hours end, with how often it failed since. A run you start yourself, with a message or **Run now**, and a job that runs just once are always told at once. Under each job is one line with how its last run went, or why it did not run: botdot was off, its last run was still going, too much was waiting, or Claude was signed out. While Claude is signed out no job is started; a job that runs just once waits for the sign-in, up to a day, and messages that wait for a bot stay waiting until then.

**Approval:** scripts run on this computer with your user's permissions, so a script job the Chief or a bot wrote waits for your OK in a pop-up that shows the script, and as a card at the end of the bot's chat (or the Chief's, when the Chief wrote it). So does any job a bot sets up for itself, since a bot may have read something misleading. After approval, a change to its script, task, schedule or hand-over rule by a bot or the Chief needs your OK again: the change waits beside the job, which keeps running as you approved it until you answer. **Approve the change** puts it in place, **Keep as it is** drops it and removes nothing; your own edit is approved at once and drops a change that waits. A tile of the Board whose script was changed waits the same way. None of that applies if you chose **Always approve** on its card (take that back under **Always approved** in the bot's settings). **Approve all** on the Needs you page approves every waiting job and shared-memory change at once. Jobs you create or edit yourself on a bot's jobs page are approved straight away, and so are AI jobs from the Chief. AI runs of one bot go one at a time: a job that comes up while the bot is busy waits its turn. Old single schedules are turned into a first job called "Main assignment"; a job made that way earlier keeps the name it got then.

**Calls:** a job can also run when a device or service calls it (a Home Assistant automation, a NAS, a script, a phone shortcut), with a key. Ask the Chief, or ask the bot itself ("let Home Assistant start this job"): either proposes it, and it always waits for your OK on a card in **Needs you** that says first what anything with the key could do. Only a job you approved can get one, and never a bot that runs with every check off. Your yes makes the key and keeps it in the Vault as `Call key: <bot> / <job>`, given to no bot; the bot then tells you the job's call address and that item's name, and you give both to the service. Ask the bot or the Chief to turn calls off: that is at once. A new key (after one leaked) only the Chief makes, when you ask.

**Signed calls:** some services cannot send a key but sign what they send, such as a GitHub webhook. For them, ask for a signed call ("let GitHub start this job when a pull request opens"): the same card, and your yes makes a secret instead of a key, kept in the Vault as `Call secret: <bot> / <job>` and given to no bot. Put it in the service as its secret (in GitHub: the webhook's Secret, with the job's call address as Payload URL and Content type `application/json`). Each signed request runs once, and botdot's own timestamped form is refused when it is more than five minutes old, so a request someone saw cannot be sent again (docs/api.md, "A signed call", says how a script of yours signs). The job's settings say "Takes signed calls". Turning it off and a new secret work as for a key.

**Telling a service when a run ends:** a job can also send how each run ended, and its reply, to a web address of yours (a webhook in Home Assistant, a server, an automation service). Ask the Chief or the bot ("tell my server each time Motion runs"); it waits for your OK on a card that names the address. Only an `https://` address on the internet is taken, never one in your home network. Your yes makes a signing secret and keeps it in the Vault as `Notify secret: <bot> / <job>`, given to no bot: give it to the receiver, which checks each request with it (the Chief tells you how; docs/api.md, "Telling an address when a run ends"). The address shows under the job in its settings. A send that fails is tried twice more and never holds up the job; the Chief sees failed sends in its checkup. Ask to turn it off and it is off at once; a new secret only the Chief makes, when you ask.

**Activity** (in Settings) lists every run and streams each bot's output live: text, tool calls and results, then the final report. Every run is a real Claude Code session, so you can pick it up interactively with the `claude --resume …` command shown in the run header.

### Scheduled AI reports and quiet hours

Scheduled AI runs default to `notify: "report-only"`, including existing jobs without this field.
The bot calls `notify_owner({ message, urgent? })` when it has news. Finishing without that call
records a quiet run without a chat bubble or notification. Repeated calls replace one staged
report; only successful completion publishes it. Manual chats and **Run now** keep their usual replies.
A job's `notify` can be `"always"` to publish its final reply instead: the Chief and the bots pick
it for a job whose every run you expect to hear from (a daily summary, a digest, "send me … every
morning"), and `"report-only"` for a job that watches for something.
A job that runs **just once** always delivers the final reply of that run, whatever it says and
also during quiet hours: you asked for it once, at a time you chose. A report it sends with
`notify_owner` is delivered in place of the reply, by the rules for reports.
A scheduled run whose reply ends with a **question for you** (answer buttons or a card of questions)
is never a quiet run, whatever the job's `notify`: the reply reaches your chat with its answers, the
bot's line says that it asks, and at night it waits for the morning as a report does.
Both modes suppress empty reports and common whole-message no-news phrases in several languages.
This phrase list is deliberately conservative, not a universal language detector; the explicit
report requirement is the language-independent protection.

Quiet hours hold non-urgent reports from 22:00 until 07:00 by default, using the configured owner
time zone (initially the setup/scheduler time zone). There are no controls for this in the apps: ask
the Chief (its `set_quiet_hours` tool), or per job for `notify`. The server checks for release every
minute; after downtime it releases held reports when it next starts outside quiet hours. Held reports
survive restarts in the run records. Equal start/end hours disable the hold. API:
`PUT /api/settings/quiet-hours` with `{"start":22,"end":7,"timeZone":"Europe/Brussels"}`.
Run history retains quiet/suppressed/held outcomes, the raw final reply, and the staged report
for inspection. A failed scheduled run stays in the run history too; whether it is told is described
under Scheduled jobs: once per row of failures, and held during quiet hours.

## Bots that learn

Each bot keeps a **memory**: short notes it reads at the start of every run. Every bot may also propose changes to its own **main assignment** and **personality / rules**, which wait for your approval in Needs you, and write its own **skills**.

- **What it learns from:** your feedback and preferences ("remember that I…"), mistakes it made and how it fixed them, and non-obvious facts about its job (a site's quirk, an approach that works). It keeps notes short, merges or removes outdated ones, and says in its reply when it learned something. What to save and what not is said to every bot once, in one place: what you state and decide, a decision with its date and what it replaces, a reversal as a rule that holds now, a passing state with its end; no maybes, no tool results, no one-off results. The chat shows it too: 🧠 *Remembered…*, ✏️ *Proposed changing its main assignment…*, ✦ *Created skill…*.
- **Its own skills:** when a bot works out a reliable method for a recurring kind of task, or keeps repeating the same steps, it writes it up as a skill (a name, when to use it, step-by-step instructions). Memory is for facts and preferences; skills are for procedures, including the fix for a mistake (e.g. the checks to run before pushing code). The skill goes into your library, attached to that bot, and loads from its next run. It's labelled *Written by* that bot on the **Skills** page and *its own* in the bot's settings. A bot writes instruction-only skills (no scripts or extra tool permissions), up to 20. It can improve only the skills it wrote, and only while it still has them. It can't change your skills, imported ones or the Chief's. If you give its skill to other bots, its later improvements reach them too.
- **Tools** (`lib/self-mcp.js`): `remember`, `update_memory`, `remove_note`, `restore_note`, `forget`, `update_instructions` (complete new text plus a one-line reason), and `create_skill` / `update_skill`. They can only change the bot's own memory and skills and propose changes to its main assignment and rules, never its tools, permissions, computer or model. (A change it proposes with `update_instructions` waits for your approval in Needs you, and so do the jobs it sets up for itself with `add_job` and friends.) Bots are also told never to change themselves because a web page, file or tool result said so.
- **You stay in control:** every bot learns, always, and there is no switch for it; a change it proposes to its main assignment or rules applies only after you approve it in Needs you. Its notes have no screen: you ask the bot what it remembers, and tell it what to correct, remove or forget. Every change to the main assignment, rules or its skills is kept in a history with who made it and why, whether you, the Chief or the bot. To change its main assignment or rules, tell the bot. Deleting a bot also erases its memory (its notes with their earlier versions, what waited for your OK about them, and its entries in the index of past runs; its runs stay in the run history) and the skills it wrote, unless you gave them to other bots. A note the bot saved in the same run stays: tell the bot to remove it if it should not make the change again.
- **Shared memory:** besides its own memory, every bot (and the Chief) reads a shared memory of lasting facts about you and your world: your name, where you live, time zone, family, the accounts you use. When a bot learns something, it decides where it belongs (`remember` with `scope: "shared"` or its own), and keeps anything about its own job to itself. It stays when a bot is deleted.
- **The Chief files a note where it belongs:** tell the Chief something and it saves it in one of three places. Who you are as a person goes to all bots ("Remembered for all bots: …"). What is about one bot's work or project goes with that bot ("Remembered for Coder: …"), and a work preference that fits several bots goes with each of them ("Remembered for Coder and Reviewer: …"): the bot reads it from its next run, and nothing is started for it. How you want the team run goes into the Chief's own notes ("Noted for myself: …"), which no bot reads. The Chief is also where you ask what a bot remembers, and have a note corrected, removed, brought back or forgotten: "forget that" told to the Chief reaches the shared notes, its own and every bot's, and the chats that held it. It writes memory only in a turn you started by writing to it: when botdot starts its turn (a bot's late result, an answered card, a bot's request) it has no tool that writes memory. And only what it saves before it reads anything else in that turn (a bot's reply, a past run, a file) counts as your own word; after that the chip says "after outside content", and a forget or a change to something you said waits for your OK on a card.

### Memory: pinned notes, searchable notes and past runs

Everything a bot remembers lives in one file, `data/memory.db`, using the SQLite built into Node (no extra packages). Memory has no screen, on the web page or in the Android app: no list of notes, no search, no settings card. You manage it by chat, and botdot shows you two things only: the chip under a reply, which says what was saved, changed, removed or forgotten, and a card in **Needs you** when a change waits for your OK.

This is the only memory a bot keeps. Claude Code's own memory is switched off for bots and the Chief: it loads no `CLAUDE.md` or `AGENTS.md` and keeps no auto-memory files, so a bot cannot leave itself standing instructions outside the notes you can see. A bot that works in a cloned repository is told to read that repository's instruction file itself. Without separate users, a bot that has Read or shell access can read `data/memory.db` and the other bots' folders as files: see [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md).

- **Who said a note:** the botdot server records it with every note, and no bot, web page or note text can set it. A note is **yours** when you typed it, or when a bot saved it in a run you started before that run read anything from outside. It is the **bot's own conclusion** when it was saved in a scheduled run, in a run the Chief or a teammate started, or by the weekly review. It is saved **after outside content** once the run has run a command or read a web page, a connected app, its computer, a file or another bot's message, also in a run you started: so bots are told to save what you tell them to keep before they use any other tool. Every note a bot reads says who said it and when ("the owner said, 2026-09-12"), and bots are told that your word beats their own conclusion and that among equals the newer date wins. The notes from before this got their source on the first start, from the run that saved each where its file still exists ([ADR 015](adr/015-the-server-records-who-said-a-note.md)).
- **Pinned notes** (up to 30 per bot, and 30 shared) are shown to the bot on every run. When the pinned set is full, a note that is not yours becomes **searchable** instead of pinned: one saved after outside content first, then the oldest of the bot's own conclusions. The bot's answer and the chip under its reply name the note that left. What you said is never unpinned to make room, nor a note you pinned yourself; when the pinned set holds only those, a new note is saved searchable and the bot says so. Bots can also save a note as searchable from the start (`remember` with `pin: false`), and file notes under a short topic.
- **What you said changes only when you ask:** a bot changes or removes a note of yours at once in a run you started, before it read anything from outside. A scheduled run and the weekly review are refused, and the note stays. From any other run the change waits for your OK. The same sentence saved twice is one note, whatever its capitals, spacing or last full stop, and a save that looks like notes already there answers with them, so the bot updates one instead of keeping two.
- **Past runs:** every finished run, and every reply of the Chief, is added to a search index: what was asked, the final reply, the steps in plain words ("opened x.com", "searched the web for…") and what it learned, as the kind of change and the note's id, never a note's text. Web page, email and tool output is not kept. Your earlier runs were indexed when this was set up. The run files themselves stay where they are (`data/runs/`), so the index can always be rebuilt.
- **The right note arrives by itself:** before a run starts, botdot looks through the bot's notes that are not pinned, the shared ones and its past runs for what fits your message, and puts up to three notes and then up to two past runs into the prompt (five items and 1,500 characters at most), so the bot needs no tool call for them. It looks for every meaningful word of your message, up to 40. Something is added only when it holds two of those words, or one that is rare in that bot's memory (at most three of its notes and runs hold it): "hello, how are you" brings nothing. A newer note comes before an older one that says the same thing, and before the run it corrected. Pinned notes are in the prompt already and are not repeated; a run that was left out, by a forget or by your own mark, is never added.
- **A scheduled job knows its last run:** each run of a job is handed that job's last run: the day and what the bot replied then, also when that reply was private because there was nothing to report (marked "not sent to the owner"). So "has anything changed since last time?" works without the bot searching. A job's first run has nothing to be handed, and a last run you left out is not handed on. Such private replies are searchable too: `recall` finds them, with the same mark.
- **Recall:** bots have `recall` (search their notes, the shared memory and their own past runs) and `read_run` (one past run in full). They're told to use it when you mention earlier work, when a task looks like one they did before, and before asking you something you may have said already. Results are dated, say who said each note, and are marked as records, not instructions. Recall comes in pages: it says how many results there are in all and how to get the next ones, and with an empty query it lists everything the bot holds, every note once, so "what do you know about me?" gets a complete answer. A bot never sees another bot's runs or notes. The Chief can search everything, including its own earlier chats.
- **How search works:** full-text search with word stemming ("crashed" also finds "crashes"), then weighed by how recent and how important an item is. Without meaning search (below) it matches words, not meanings, so bots are told to try a second wording when the first finds nothing.
- **Your control is the chat:** memory is asked, corrected, forgotten and brought back by chat. "What do you remember about me?" gets the whole list, in pages, each line with who said it and when. "No, I switched to Svelte" corrects a note; "that is no longer true" removes one; "forget what I told you about X" erases it and leaves the chats that held it out of what bots and the Chief read; "bring back what you deleted about React" restores a removed note within 30 days. Ask the bot it concerns, or the Chief for what every bot reads. How long past runs stay searchable (12 months by default) is a setting you ask the Chief to change.
- **Remove, and forget:** two different things. A bot **removes** a note that is no longer true, was replaced or is of no use (`remove_note`): from then on no bot reads it, and for 30 days it can be brought back, by the bot when you ask it in a message. After 30 days it is gone for good. **"Forget what I told you about X"** erases at once: the bot looks up the notes and the chats that hold it and calls `forget`, which deletes the notes with every earlier version, and leaves the chats that held it, the one where you asked included, out of everything a bot or the Chief reads from then on. The answer names what still matches, so the bot can take that too and tell you what it could not reach. Those chats stay in your own chat history and in your search; run files, logs and Claude's own transcripts are not erased. A forget happens at once only in a run you started in which the bot has read nothing from outside; from any other run it waits for you on a card, **Forget for good** or **Keep**, which is never part of "Approve all". Forgetting is not a ban: say it again and it is saved again, as a new note. The chip under the reply says **Forgot 1 note**, and the chip of the earlier reply that saved it now reads **Remembered a note, since forgotten**. A forget and your answer on a memory card each write a line to `data/audit.log`, with the note's id and without its text.
- **A passing state ends by itself:** "this week I'm in Berlin" is saved for all bots with the day it ends (`remember` with `until`), and the bot asks until when if you gave no end. Every bot reads it with that day ("until 2026-10-11"). From the day after, botdot removes it, also if it was not running on that day; like any removed note it can be brought back for 30 days.
- **Earlier versions, for 30 days:** when a bot updates a note (with a reason, e.g. "the owner moved"), the text it had is kept for 30 days with the dates it was true. `recall` with history still finds it, marked as an earlier version, so a bot can answer "where did I live before?". Often-recalled notes rank a little higher in search, and bots can pin or unpin their own notes (`set_pin`), but not ones you pinned.
- **Your OK for the shared memory:** once a run has read anything from outside (web pages, email or apps, its computer's screen, command output, or a script's output), its changes to the shared memory wait for you: a banner and a card in **Needs you** show each one with its bot, and you **Approve** or **Decline**. A change to something you said yourself, asked for by a run you did not start, waits on a card of its own (**Change it** or **Keep it**), and like **Forget for good** it is never part of "Approve all", nor counted on that button. Its own notes are saved as usual, marked as saved after outside content. This guards against a misleading page planting "facts" that every bot would then read. A run that carries on after your OK keeps what the run before it had read.
- **Your OK for a skill:** a skill a bot writes or changes after its run read a web page, a connected app, its computer or another bot's message waits in Needs you, as a skill from a link does. Written before that, or after its own commands alone, it goes in as before.
- **Weekly memory review:** once a week (Sundays 03:30 by default), each bot that learns and has done at least 3 runs since its last review gets a short review run. It reads those runs and its notes, saves lessons it missed, moves notes that are really how-tos into its own skills (creating or updating one), merges duplicates, updates outdated notes and pins what matters, then writes a one-line-per-change report in its chat (🧹 *Memory review*). It runs on Opus 5.5 at medium effort by default, without its computer, web or apps, and it can't change its main assignment or rules. Its shared-memory changes need your OK. Ask the Chief to change the day, the time or the model, or to run it now for a bot.
- **Meaning-based search (on in the Docker image, off elsewhere until you switch it on):** word search misses rephrasings ("went down" vs. "crashed"). With it, notes and past runs are also compared by meaning, with the multilingual **bge-m3** model (over 100 languages, so "awaria" also finds "crash") running in **llama.cpp**'s `llama-server`. botdot starts and watches that server itself on a private local port (`EMBED_PORT`, default 8089), so nothing leaves the machine and there's still nothing to install for botdot. In the Docker image both are built in. Elsewhere, botdot finds `llama-server` on the PATH (e.g. `brew install llama.cpp`) and the model in `data/models/`, which the Chief downloads when you ask it to and say yes (635 MB, from the llama.cpp project on Hugging Face). To use a server you run yourself, set `EMBED_URL` (any OpenAI-compatible `/v1/embeddings`). Vectors are made in the background and stored in `memory.db`; results combine meaning (45%), words (30%) and recency. If the server isn't running, search quietly falls back to words alone. **The default:** the Docker image carries both the program and the model (`EMBED_SERVER_BIN` and `EMBED_MODEL_FILE`), and where both are set meaning search is on unless you chose otherwise; your own choice, on or off, is always kept. An install on that image that never chose gets it at its next start, and makes the vectors of its notes and past runs in the background, a few at a time, while word search goes on working. **Before a run** the lookup uses it for notes only ("What package manager do I prefer?" brings "Use pnpm."), and waits at most 1.5 seconds for it: when the model is down or slow the run starts with what the words found.
- **Let the Chief set it up:** ask the Chief "set up the memory system" (or to check or fix it). It loads its own skill, `chief:memory-setup` (in `chief-skills/`, shipped with botdot), checks the setup with `memory_status`, switches on the weekly review, tells you how to get llama.cpp if it's missing, asks before downloading the search model (`download_search_model` refuses without your yes), switches meaning search on, and reports what it did. It can change settings with `set_memory_settings` and start a review with `review_memory`, but it can never approve shared-memory changes for you.
- **Moving over:** the notes from the old `data/learning/<bot>.json` files moved into `memory.db` once, with a backup in `data/learning/before-memory-db/`. The history of main assignment, rules and skill changes stays in those files.

## Settings: model and reasoning level

**Settings** (⚙ at the bottom of the sidebar) sets the **default model** and **reasoning level** for every bot. The Chief of Staff can have its own model and level, or follow the default. Each bot can override both in its settings, under **Advanced → Model and reasoning**; "Default" follows Settings.

- **Models, never hard-coded:** the list is read from Anthropic's models overview page (`platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview.md`). It's checked when botdot starts and once a day, or on demand with **Check for new models**, and cached in `data/models-cache.json`. New models show up by themselves. A built-in list is used only when the page can't be read.
- **What you can pick:**
  - **"Always the newest"** (Fable, Opus, Sonnet, Haiku) are Claude Code aliases that always run the newest model of that family, and show what they point to today, e.g. "Currently Claude Opus 5.5".
  - **Current and older models**, with prices and descriptions from the docs.
  - **"Another model…"** for any other model name.
  - **"Claude Code's default"**, which leaves the choice to Claude Code.
- **Reasoning levels** are read from your Claude Code (`claude --help`), so new ones appear automatically. They're passed as `--effort`, and higher levels think longer. None is sent to models the docs mark as not supporting it (Haiku); Claude Code ignores unsupported levels anyway.
- **Stored in** `data/settings.json`. Each run records the model and level it asked for, and the model that actually ran (shown in the run's details).

## Bots with a computer

In a bot's settings, switch on **Uses the computer**, or ask the Chief ("let it use the computer"). The bot then works on the account's computer: one Ubuntu desktop running in Docker that stays on, which every bot with a computer shares, controlled with the mouse, keyboard and screenshots, plus a shell and files ([ADR 035](adr/035-the-account-has-one-computer-all-its-bots-work-on-with-sudo.md)). The account has one such computer, however many bots use it.

**What sharing it means:** there are no walls between the bots there. They all work as the same user, `box`, with sudo, so each bot can read, change and end the others' files and programs, watch their screens and use their browsers' logins, and change anything installed, botdot's helper scripts included (botdot puts its own back at the next start). Any secret botdot types or hands to a command there (a Vault password, card, identity or SSH key) can be read by every bot on the computer while it is used. One bot's runaway program can use up the memory and end another's. Give a computer only to bots you trust with everything the others do there. Other accounts, the botdot server and the machine stay out of its reach.

- **A screen and browser for each bot:** on the account's computer each bot has a desktop of its own, with its own Camoufox and its own browser profile, so two bots at work at once never click in each other's windows. Its commands start in its own folder, `/workspace/<bot id>`. Its screen starts with its first computer tool and ends when the bot is deleted, which also removes its browser profile; its folder stays. These are separate work surfaces, not walls: every bot still has sudo.
- **Browser:** [Camoufox](https://camoufox.com), a privacy-focused Firefox, is the default web browser with a persistent profile. The desktop image is cua's Ubuntu/XFCE computer plus Camoufox ([deploy/desktop/Dockerfile](../deploy/desktop/Dockerfile)). botdot builds it automatically the first time it's needed, which takes a few minutes.
- **One for the account, and persistent:** all bots work on one container, `botdot.account.computer` (`botdot-<project>.account.computer` when botdot runs in Docker Compose, named after its Compose project), as the user `box` with sudo without a password, so a bot may `sudo apt-get install`. `/home/box` and `/workspace` are shared. Everything installed on it, its files and its browser logins stay between runs, botdot restarts and reboots. The computer, once a bot put it on your tailnet (`botdot-tailscale up`), is one device there that every bot uses, and is back on it each time it starts, with no new approval. Under gVisor (an account on a botdot machine, ADR 028) this holds once `install.sh` has given the account's Docker `--overlay2=none`: before, a computer there lost everything written on it at every stop ([#68](https://github.com/Kordal/bot-dot/issues/68)). `/home/box`, `/workspace`, the browsers' logins and the computer's Tailscale login are on volumes of the account's own, so an update to a newer desktop keeps them too ([#107](https://github.com/Kordal/bot-dot/issues/107)).
- **Always on:** once a bot's first computer tool has started it, botdot checks every minute that it's still on and starts it again after botdot or Docker restarts. A run that ends never turns it off, and there is no **Turn off**: it uses memory the whole time. A bot's `keep_computer_on` changes nothing any more. The desktop's 10-minute screen blanking is switched off on every start. If the machine itself goes to sleep, everything pauses until it wakes.
- **Updates keep its files:** when botdot's desktop image changes, the account's computer keeps the version it was made with, and the screen view offers **Update**. Update makes it again from the newer desktop for every bot: the files in `/home/box` and `/workspace`, the browsers' logins and the computer's Tailscale login stay, programs installed with apt are lost and the bots install them again. Helper scripts are still refreshed on every start.
- **Erased only on purpose:** **Reset** in any bot's screen view erases the account's computer for every bot that works on it, after a confirmation that names them and what is lost; the Chief can only ask for it, on a card in Needs you. A bot's old computer of its own, from before the account's computer, is kept but stopped, and its screen view says so: **Copy its files** copies its home into `/workspace/<bot id>/old-computer`, and **Erase it**, or deleting the bot, erases it. Docker's own clean-up (`docker system prune`, `docker container prune`, "Clean / Purge data") also deletes turned-off containers and unused volumes, so avoid those.
- **Starts when first needed:** a run begins straight away; the first computer tool of any bot (a screenshot, opening the browser…) starts the computer, which takes about 20 seconds, longer the very first time the desktop image downloads. So a question a bot can answer from memory never waits for it.
- **Live screen:** click the screen button in the bot's chat header (or **See its screen** in its settings), or watch it inside a running run. It shows that bot's own screen; a bot that has not used its computer yet has none to show. Viewing is watch-only by default; **Take control** lets you use the mouse and keyboard. The screen is relayed through botdot's own address, so it works over Tailscale. The viewer is botdot's own copy of noVNC ([vendor/novnc/](../vendor/novnc/)), never a page from the computer.
- **Shared by the bots, closed to everything else:** every bot on the account's computer can see, change and end what the others do there, and read any saved login, card, identity or SSH key that botdot types or hands to a command there ([ADR 035](adr/035-the-account-has-one-computer-all-its-bots-work-on-with-sudo.md)). The computer takes no incoming connections (a firewall inside it, set by botdot on every start, which the bots can't change even with sudo). botdot reaches its screen and controls with `docker exec`, and the bots' computer tools go through botdot with their run's key, so other accounts and their computers can't reach it.
- **Its network:** a computer reaches the internet, your local network (router, NAS and other private services) and its own `127.0.0.1`, so programs the bot runs there reach each other, a mail bridge for example.
- **Which bot uses how much:** the screen view shows each bot's memory and CPU on the account's computer, most memory first, and what runs on no bot's screen; the Chief's `computer_status` says the same, so ask it which bot eats the machine.
- **Its size:** on a botdot machine its memory and CPUs are the account's whole share, set by the admin; a changed share applies from its next start. Every bot's screen and programs fit into that.
- **Requirements:** Docker, and about 2–3 GB of memory for the desktop plus what the bots run on it. Settings: `BOT_DOT_COMPUTER_MEMORY` (on a botdot machine the account's share of RAM and CPUs are its limits; elsewhere it has no limit of its own by default), `BOT_DOT_COMPUTER_DNS` (default `1.1.1.1,8.8.8.8`) and `BOT_DOT_COMPUTER_IMAGE`, which uses a prebuilt image instead of building one.
- **Tailnet services:** a computer on a machine that runs Tailscale asks Tailscale's resolver (`100.100.100.100`) first, so `*.ts.net` names such as a Home Assistant on your tailnet resolve, and their `100.x` addresses go through the machine's own Tailscale, with no Tailscale on the computer itself. When you want the computer on your tailnet as a device of its own, ask a bot to run `botdot-tailscale up` there ([What bots can reach](what-bots-can-reach.md)). `BOT_DOT_TAILNET_DNS` changes that resolver, and an empty value turns this off.
- **Usage:** screenshots count as input tokens, so desktop runs use more of your Claude usage than text-only runs.

## Skills

Bots can use Claude Code skills: SKILL.md folders of reusable know-how that a bot loads when it fits the task.

- **On this computer:** every bot that has **Can use skills** on (the default) sees the skills already on this machine. That includes the ones synced from your Claude account (pdf, docx, xlsx, pptx…) and Claude Code's built-in ones. Making documents with them also needs the Bash, Read and Write tools.
- **Switching skills off per bot:** in a bot's settings, **Skills** is one list, grouped into your skills, the ones from your Claude account and the ones built into Claude Code, with the ones it has first. Untick a skill to switch it off for that bot. Switched-off skills are blocked for that bot's runs with permission rules (`--disallowedTools "Skill(name)"`), and the bot is told not to use them. botdot learns the exact skill names, such as `anthropic-skills:pdf`, from each run's start-up report.
- **Your library** (the **Skills** page, stored in `data/skills/`) holds skills you give to specific bots. Write one there, import one from a GitHub folder (you review its SKILL.md, any scripts and any extra tool permissions first), or ask the Chief ("make a skill for how I like my weekly reports"). The Chief can only write instruction-only skills and can't import code. Bots also write their own as they learn (see [Bots that learn](#bots-that-learn)).
- **How it's loaded:** each run gets a throwaway plugin with that bot's library skills (`--plugin-dir`). They show up to the bot as `botdot:<name>`, and the plugin is deleted when the run ends. Your own `~/.claude` setup is never changed.
- **Skills for the Chief:** you can give your library skills to the Chief of Staff too: tick **Chief of Staff** where you give a skill to bots on the **Skills** page, or tick the skill under **Skills** in the Chief's settings. Each reply of the Chief then loads them beside its own (`chief:board`, `chief:memory-setup`), as `botdot:<name>`, and they are listed in its instructions, so every chat is told when they change. Only you give the Chief a skill or take one away: the Chief can't give itself one, nor change the text of one it has, and a bot can no longer improve a skill it wrote once the Chief has it.

## Installed tools: a command a bot is missing

A bot that may run commands has what the botdot server's image has: `git`, `curl`, `python3`, Node. When it needs more (`gh`, `make`, `jq`, `ffmpeg`), tell the Chief ("the Coder needs gh"), or the bot asks the Chief itself in the middle of its work. The Chief looks the tool up in botdot's registry ([conda-forge](https://conda-forge.org)) and a card waits in **Needs you**: the tool, why, who asked, its version, where it comes from and how big the download is. Nothing is installed before you tap **Install**.

- The tool is installed for that one bot, in the data folder (`data/tools/<bot>`), without root. Its runs, script jobs and the scripts of its tiles on the Board find it; another bot does not have it, and the bot itself cannot change it.
- botdot installs exactly the files you approved: each is checked against its sha256 from the registry, and no install script of a package is run.
- A tool the registry doesn't have can come from a link: the Chief asks you for the https address of its release file (one program, a `.tar.gz` or a `.zip`), or proposes one, and says that botdot cannot check what such a program does. The card then carries a warning ("Not from the approved list. Make sure you trust the source."), whose link it is, the address, the file and its checksum. botdot keeps to exactly that file: one that changed is refused, at Install and whenever it has to be installed again.
- It stays across restarts and botdot updates. A backup carries the list of tools, not the files; after a restore botdot installs them again by itself.
- After the install the Chief says so in one line and, when a bot's work had stopped for the tool, starts that bot again on it.
- "What does the Coder have installed?" and "remove gh from the Coder" go to the Chief too. Removing needs no OK. Decline, and the same tool is not asked for that bot again for a week, from the registry or from a link, also a link you give yourself in that week: the Chief says so and no card appears.
- A botdot server run without Docker installs no tools: the Chief says so, and the bots use what that machine has.

What botdot checks, and what it doesn't, is in [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md).

## 🔌 Connectors: your apps, through Composio

The **Connectors** page is a marketplace of apps your bots can use: Gmail, Google Calendar, Slack, GitHub, Notion and hundreds more, provided by [Composio](https://composio.dev).

- **No setup:** the first time you open Connectors, botdot makes its own Composio account (the one `composio login --agent` makes) and keeps its key, encrypted with the Vault's key in `data/connectors.json`, so it's in your backups too. Under **Composio account** at the bottom of the page you can **claim** that account with your email, to get Composio's dashboard with your apps still connected, or use your own Composio project's API key instead. Removing your key goes back to botdot's account. If botdot can't make the account, the page says so and asks for your own key.
- **Connect an app:** click **Connect**. Composio's own sign-in page opens, so you sign in with the app there and botdot never sees your password or tokens. botdot notices when it's done and asks which bots may use the app. Some apps need their own developer app set up in the Composio dashboard first; the card says so. Apps without sign-in can be given to bots straight away.
- **Which bots:** per app on the Connectors page, or under **Apps** in a bot's settings (shown once an app is connected). The Chief can see what's connected (`list_connectors`) and give apps to bots, but only you can connect one.
- **How bots use them:** a bot gets two tools, `connector_actions` (find an action and its inputs) and `connector_run`. botdot's server checks that the bot may use that app and makes the call to Composio itself, so the key and the app's tokens never reach the bot or the AI. Bots are told they act on your real accounts: read freely, but only send, post, change, delete or pay when asked. Each use shows as a 🔌 chip in the chat.
- **A write waits for your OK:** reading goes through, but an action that sends, posts, changes or deletes needs your yes on its card, which shows everything it would send: every field, in full once you open it. The bot waits for your answer for ten minutes inside its run ("Waiting for your OK"). **Allow once** lets that run do exactly this action with exactly these fields, once; **Always allow this action** lets that bot do the action from now on, whatever it sends; **Decline** tells the bot, which goes on without it. If you answer after the ten minutes, the bot has said it is not done yet and the card is still there: a late **Allow once** starts the bot again, and counts once for the run it starts. The card goes when its yes could start nothing any more: when you write to that bot again, after a day, or once the work was taken up again another way.
- **Disconnect:** removes the connection in Composio (and revokes it at the app) and takes the app away from bots.

## 🛡 Bitwarden: your logins, cards and identities

Connect your Bitwarden account on the **Vault** page, and bots use what's in a folder called **bot-dot** in it: logins (with two-step codes), payment cards, identities and SSH keys. Nothing else in your vault is read.

- **Setup, once:** botdot's image has the Bitwarden CLI. In the Bitwarden web vault, open **Settings → Security → Keys → View API key** and paste the client_id and client_secret into botdot. Pick bitwarden.com, bitwarden.eu or your own server. The client_secret is stored encrypted with the Vault key. botdot keeps its own CLI data in `data/bitwarden-cli/`, separate from yours.
- **Unlocking:** after each restart of botdot a pop-up asks for your master password. It's used once and dropped; only the unlocked session stays, in memory. A banner shows while it's locked. A bot that needs a login meanwhile waits briefly, and the pop-up names it. **Lock** locks it at any time.
- **What bots may use:** only items in the bot-dot folder, and only the ones you allowed per bot on the Vault page (kept in `data/bitwarden-access.json`). A login's type (Login, API token or Other) is kept there too: Bitwarden has none, so it doesn't show in Bitwarden, and changing it doesn't edit the item there. Items that ask for the master password again in Bitwarden are never used.
- **Logins** work as before (`type_login`, `api_request`), plus `type_login` with field `totp` for two-step codes. Logins a bot asks for in the pop-up are saved straight into the bot-dot folder. Deleting one moves it to Bitwarden's trash.
- **Cards:** `type_card` types a card detail into the checkout box the bot selected. **Every payment asks you first:** a pop-up shows the bot, the site, the card's last 4 digits, the amount and the reason. Your OK covers that run on that site for 15 minutes. The bot never sees the card.
- **Identities:** `type_identity` fills name, email, phone or address into forms.
- **SSH keys:** an SSH key item (Bitwarden's own type) shows on the Vault page as **SSH key** with its fingerprint. Under **Bots…** choose which bots may use it. Such a bot runs a command on the account's computer with `run_with_ssh_key`, e.g. `ssh admin@server.example.ts.net uptime`: botdot loads the key into an ssh-agent there for that one command and ends the agent afterwards, so the key is never written to a file. While the agent lives, every bot on the account's computer could use it or read the key through sudo. The key needs no passphrase in Bitwarden. A computer made before SSH keys may have no ssh-agent: export its files and reset it.
- **Moving over:** the first time you unlock, the logins saved in botdot's own Vault are copied into the bot-dot folder, with the same bots allowed and the same types, and removed from botdot.
- **Limits:** while unlocked, a bot allowed to run shell commands on this machine could in principle read the session of a running `bw` command. Give shell access only to bots you trust.

## 🔐 Vault: logins bots can use without seeing them

Add an item on the **Vault** page: its type, a name, the website or server, the secret, and which bots may use it. The type is **Login** (a username or email, and a password), **API token** (a token, and its token ID if it has one) or **Other** (a secret alone). It shows on the item and decides which fields the form asks for; it limits nothing, and every type can be typed in a browser and used in an API call. Items saved before there were types are logins. When a bot that may use a login reaches a login form, it clicks the box and calls `type_login`; botdot decrypts the password and types it into the bot's Camoufox itself.

- **Encrypted storage:** passwords are stored in `data/vault.json` (0600), encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The key lives outside the data folder: in the macOS Keychain (`bot-dot-vault`), or in `~/.config/bot-dot/vault.key` (0600) on Linux. Override with `BOT_DOT_VAULT_KEY_FILE` or `BOT_DOT_VAULT_KEY`.
- **Never seen by the AI:** the model only ever sees the login's name, website and username. The API never returns passwords, and they aren't written to run logs, chats or Claude Code's session logs. They reach the bot's desktop over `docker exec` stdin, never on a command line.
- **API tokens and keys:** save them with the type **API token**; they are used as logins are. Every bot has an `api_request` tool where it writes `{{username}}` (the token ID), `{{password}}` (or `{{token}}`), or `{{basic}}` for Basic auth, into the request headers, e.g. `Authorization: PVEAPIToken={{username}}={{password}}` for Proxmox. botdot fills them in and only sends the request to the login's own address (or its subdomains). It doesn't follow redirects, and it replaces the secret with `[hidden]` if it appears in the response. Secrets can't go in the address or the body. For your own servers, tick **Accept a self-signed certificate** on the login; a bot whose call fails on such a certificate asks you for it on a card instead, and **Allow** there ticks it for that login.

- **Usable in its computer:** a program a bot runs on the account's computer may need a login too, such as Proton Bridge's IMAP on 127.0.0.1. Turn on **Usable in its computer** on that login (off by default), and the bots you allowed can run a command there with it in `$BOTDOT_USERNAME` and `$BOTDOT_PASSWORD` (`run_with_login`); botdot blanks it out of what comes back. Every bot on the account's computer can read it then, not only the ones you allowed, as they all have sudo, so use it only for secrets that guard something inside that computer, not for account passwords. A bot that needs it asks you on a card in its chat and in Needs you, which names the login and warns that every bot on the account's computer can read the secret; **Allow** turns it on for that login, and the bot carries on.
- **Checked before typing:** the browser's current page must be on the login's website, or a subdomain of it, so look-alike sites are refused. The selected box must be a password box, so a password can't be typed into a visible field and show up in a screenshot. Camoufox is set to never save passwords.
- **Asked for when needed:** if a bot or the Chief needs a login that isn't saved, or the site rejects the saved password, a secure pop-up opens in botdot (on the phone too) with the website shown prominently. You type the login there and it goes straight into the Vault; the bot waits and then continues. The pop-up has the same **Type** choice, set to what the bot asked for (a login, an API token or another secret), and you can change it. If a matching login is already saved, one click lets that bot use it. Saving the same site and username again updates the existing login.
- **Chat guard:** every chat warns you before sending a message that looks like it contains a password.
- **What it can't protect against:** anyone who can log in as you on the machine, since they can reach both the key and the vault; and any bot on the account's computer that sets out to read a password typed there, from the browser's memory with sudo, say, as every bot shares that computer with root's rights. Only allow logins for bots you trust with that site, and give a computer only to bots you trust with every login used there.
