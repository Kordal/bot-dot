# Russian words

The Russian glossary: [lib/i18n/GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md) says what each English term is in botdot. The same words are used in the dictionary, in the What's new notes in `whats-new/ru/` and in the Play listing (`docs/google-play/listing-ru.md`).

## How the interface speaks

- **The owner is "вы", lowercase** ("ваши боты", "ждёт вас"), never "ты". The shared glossary suggests the informal form, but the two apps Russian speakers use most, Telegram and VK, both say "вы" to their users, as do Яндекс, Госуслуги, Авито and the banks; "ты" is the voice of a few youth brands and of ads, and in a tool that holds one's bots, passwords and accounts it reads as overfamiliar. Lowercase "вы", not the letter's capital "Вы", as those apps write it.
- **A button or a menu item is an infinitive**: "Сохранить", "Удалить", "Открыть", "Отменить" (or the noun "Отмена" for Cancel), as Android, Telegram and every Russian app have them. A switch is an infinitive too ("Обновлять автоматически"). A heading is a noun ("Настройки", "Обновления"). A state or a short note is a past participle or a short adjective ("Сохранено", "Готово").
- **Ё is written** ("ждёт", "подключённые", "закреплённая"): it is the correct spelling and spares the reader a guess.
- **Counts** use the three forms: one ("{n} бот", 1, 21, 31…), few ("{n} бота", 2–4, 22–24…), many ("{n} ботов", 0, 5–20, 25…), and other for fractions ("{n} бота"). A count is never written as "бот(ов)".
- **Quotes** are «ёлочки» in sentences; a name of a button inside a sentence is in «» too.
- **Dates and times** are 24-hour, "d MMMM" ("26 ноября").
- **Names stay as they are**, in Latin letters and not declined where Russian apps leave them undeclined: botdot (always lowercase, "в botdot", "botdot обновлён"), Claude, Claude Code, Gmail, Composio, boat.dev, Bitwarden, Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, GitHub, Google Play, Android, iPhone, Tailscale.
