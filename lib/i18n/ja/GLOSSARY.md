# Japanese words

The Japanese glossary: [lib/i18n/GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md) says what each English term is in botdot. The same words in the dictionary, the What's new notes in `whats-new/ja/` and the Play listing (`docs/google-play/listing-ja.md`).

- **Style:** the polite but plain です/ます of Japanese apps for every sentence to the owner ("保存できませんでした", "ボットが作業中です"). No honorific excess (no ご〜いただけます, no させていただきます). The owner is rarely named: Japanese leaves "you" out. Where it must be said, "あなた".
- **Buttons and menu items:** a short noun or a verb stem, as Japanese apps use them: 保存, 削除, 送信, キャンセル, 再接続, 今すぐアップデート. A heading is a noun. A short status note is a past form: 保存しました, コピーしました.
- **Punctuation:** Japanese 、 and 。; 「」 for a quote; full-width （） and ： in a Japanese sentence. No space between Japanese and a Latin word or a `{placeholder}` ("{name}を実行しました", "Gmailと連携").
- **Counts:** Japanese has one plural category, `other`. A bot is counted with 体 (as a robot or a character): "ボット{n}体", "{n}体のボット"; a thing waiting or a message with 件; a minute 分, an hour 時間, a day 日.
- **Katakana:** the long vowel mark as Microsoft and current Japanese apps write it: コンピューター, ユーザー, フォルダー, サーバー, アップデート, パラメーター.
- **Names stay as they are:** botdot (lowercase), Claude, Claude Code, Gmail, Composio, boat.dev, Bitwarden, Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, GitHub, Google Play, Android, iPhone.

## Sentences built from parts

- "Couldn't {what}" is "{what}に失敗しました", so every `what` (save it, share it, rename it…) is a noun: 保存, 共有, 名前の変更.
- "Let {bot} {what}?" is "{bot}に{what}を許可しますか？", so a permission's label is a noun phrase: Webの閲覧, コマンドの実行.
- "Asks to {what}" is "許可を求めています：{what}", with the same nouns.
- A schedule puts the days before the time, with no particle: 平日 09:00, 毎週月曜日 10:00; a span is 〜.
