# Turkish words

The Turkish glossary: [lib/i18n/GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md) says what each English term is in botdot. The same words as the What's new notes in `whats-new/tr/` and the Play listing (`docs/google-play/listing-tr.md`).

**How the owner is addressed:** informal "sen", as Instagram, Spotify, Duolingo and Getir speak to people in Turkish: "Botunu seç", "Senin için", "Seni bekliyor". "Sen" and "senin" are lowercase in a sentence.

**Buttons and menu items:** the bare imperative, the form every Turkish app uses ("Kaydet", "Sil", "Gönder", "Şimdi güncelle"). A heading is a noun ("Ayarlar", "Etkinlik"). A state is a short verb form ("Çalışıyor", "Kaydedildi").

**Names stay as they are:** botdot (always lowercase), Claude, Claude Code, Gmail, Composio, boat.dev, Bitwarden, Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, GitHub, Google Play. A suffix on a name takes an apostrophe and follows the name's last vowel: "botdot'un", "Claude'a", "Gmail'e". A title such as "Şef" takes its suffix without one ("Şefe yaz", "Şefin notu"), as Turkish writes "Başkana".

**Placeholders:** a `{name}` can end in any vowel, so a suffix right after it can't follow vowel harmony. Put the suffix on a noun after it ("{bot} botuna", "{name} adlı grup"), keep the placeholder in the nominative ("{bot}: yanıt yok"), or reorder the sentence. A number takes no suffix in a placeholder: "{n} bot", and a noun after a number stays singular ("3 bot", "5 mesaj"), so a plural's `one` and `other` are usually the same text.

**Dotted and dotless i:** "İ/i" and "I/ı" are different letters. Nothing in this dictionary is written in capitals for show; a word that starts a heading starts with "İ" where it has "i" ("İş", "İzin ver").
