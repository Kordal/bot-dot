# Indonesian words

The Indonesian glossary: [lib/i18n/GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md) says what each English term is in botdot. The same words go in the What's new notes in `whats-new/id/` and the Play listing (`docs/google-play/listing-id.md`).

- **The owner is "kamu"**, as Gojek, Tokopedia and Shopee talk to their users: "kamu", "-mu" ("botmu", "Menunggumu"), never "Anda". Plain, friendly sentences, no "silakan" padding.
- **A button or menu item is a bare imperative verb**: Simpan, Hapus, Batal, Kirim, Tambah, Buka. A heading is a noun: Pengaturan, Aktivitas, Pembaruan.
- **A state or a short note is a passive or adjective**: Tersimpan, Tersalin, Terhubung, Selesai.
- **Loanwords stay where Indonesian app users really say them**: bot, file, folder, browser, skill, link, login, email, chat, admin, server, update (only as "Perbarui" on a button), standup, keypad, token, PIN. "Pesan" for a message, "obrolan" is not used: the chat is "chat".
- **Names stay as they are**: botdot (always lowercase), Claude, Claude Code, Gmail, Composio, boat.dev, Bitwarden, Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, GitHub, Google Play. Indonesian has no case endings, so a name never changes; "-mu" and "-nya" join a common noun only ("botmu", never "Alfredmu").
- **Plurals**: Indonesian has one form ("other"). A count needs no plural ending: "3 bot", "1 bot". Reduplication ("bot-bot") is not used in the interface.
- **Dates and numbers** follow `id-ID`: "Senin, 26 November", 1.234,5.
